Industries

Revalidation without the redraw.

Hazards checked against as-built, not archive. Deviations surfaced. Decisions defensible. Chemicals plants carry the industry’s heaviest review calendar, and the drawings that calendar depends on are the ones the archive cannot vouch for.

The pressure, Change density

The change never stops.

Product switches, campaign changes, feedstock swaps: a chemicals plant reconfigures itself more often than any refinery, and every reconfiguration lands a change request on the same engineers who own the hazard reviews. MOC density climbs. The as-built drifts from the archive. And the next revalidation inherits the gap: a hazard study run against drawings the plant no longer matches.

Management of change

Workflows for chemicals

The revalidation, run on the as-built.

Cortex compiles the plant as it is: the drawings, the documents, and the historian, connected, in under 48 hours. The hazard study runs against that graph, not the archive, and the deviations between the two surface as findings instead of surprises.

01

Compile the as-built

The full drawing set and document base become one knowledge graph. Where the sheets disagree with each other or with the field record, the disagreement is flagged, not buried.

02

Draft the study

Nodes, deviations, and consequences drafted from the graph, each cited to the sheet and the tag. The panel receives a prepared study, not a blank worksheet.

03

Review the exceptions

Your experts spend their sessions on the deviations that need judgment: the flagged gaps, the novel scenarios, the consequences worth debating.

04

Close the loop

Findings route into change requests with their evidence attached, and the record shows what was checked, against what revision, by whom.

The standard

Defensible means cited.

A hazard decision is only as strong as the drawing it was made against. Every claim an Intuigent makes carries its source: the sheet, the tag, the document revision. When it cannot verify something, it says so instead of guessing. That is what a defensible record looks like.
